This repo contains memory of continuous analysis and prioritisation of book recommendations found online.

The task of the agent it to digest the input link(s) and perform the analysis of the information available on that link page, which my include main information part and many auxiliary data and comments all of which should be considered.

File SOURCES.md contains a single table with the input links to be analysed, with the index in first column, date in second column, the link in third, and output analysis file name in forth.

File RECOMMENDED_BOOKS.md contains the main content of the repo, a single table of books recommended and amended after every analysis. First column is the date of entry, second is the the book publishing year, third is book author, then book title, then the score, and finaly the number of the source link as provided in the table in file SOURCES.md

File VETOED_BOOKS.md lists every currently vetoed source book once, with all contributing source numbers, the veto reason and confidence, and the full counterfactual criterion breakdown, thematic deduction, and total. Counterfactual scores suspend only the veto gate, retain the identified flaws, and do not make a book recommended. Hard-excluded books and non-book references belong in neither RECOMMENDED_BOOKS.md nor VETOED_BOOKS.md.

File EXCLUDED_BOOKS.md is the single cumulative list of hard-excluded source books under either exclusion rule. Add every future hard exclusion here once, identified by title and author when known. Use one alphabetized bullet per work, with no scores or rating columns. Put the exclusion reason and evidence in the relevant analysis; retain a short identity qualification in the list when needed to distinguish works. Do not add non-book references or titles absent from the source material. Check this file during every analysis, and reassess an earlier exclusion if new evidence changes the book's identity or eligibility. A book must appear in at most one of EXCLUDED_BOOKS.md, RECOMMENDED_BOOKS.md and VETOED_BOOKS.md. When its status changes, remove the obsolete entry and preserve prior analysis files as history.

Files analysis_<short-src>_<date> contains detailed output of every specific analysis of the source. For every input link it should provide an overview of the thinking process, final recommended set with the details of the scoring, and other useful or extreme info such as which books were the worst and especially unfitting to be recommended. Majority of the format detail is part of the main analysis prompt. "short-src" is short readable abriviation of input source link text up to 12 characters so make it specific and context relevant, and date uses YYMMDD_HHMM: a two-digit year, month, day, underscore, hour and minute, with no T, seconds or timezone suffix. Use Europe/Warsaw local time. Store analysis files in the analysis/ folder. The full path is analysis/analysis_<short-src>_YYMMDD_HHMM.md.

Main analysis guidelines are below. They are to be an input for every analysis and provide detailed instructions on collecting, filtering, scoring, judging, and formatting the final result. This prompt is the most relevant part of this page and use is in its strict form and prescribed detail with almost mathematical rigor.

## Hard exclusion rules - a book is excluded and not even considered vetoed:
1. Works which do not represent a book and are never published in a book form.
2. Exclude fiction books and do not consider them vetoed. Discard them before scoring or further analysis.
3. Technical training and reference exclusion. Exclude works whose main purpose is to teach a technical curriculum, develop specialist proficiency, present formal proofs or derivations, provide procedures, or serve as a technical reference. Clear prose, historical introductions, conceptual explanations, prestige, and absence of exercises do not override this exclusion. Allow technically demanding books when their main purpose is sustained explanation of discoveries, mechanisms, decisions, or historical development, and their main intellectual value can be obtained without completing a course of technical study. Historical scientific treatises remain excluded when their main content requires reconstruction of specialist proofs, notation, or obsolete technical methods. Historical importance alone does not establish eligibility. Historical surveys and annotated chronologies can qualify when substantive explanatory prose provides value beyond locating references.
4. Exclude books not available in English or Croatian language.

Apply both hard exclusions before vetoes or scoring, including to profile examples and books previously recommended or vetoed. Scores, reader ratings, author standing and the biography exceptions under V3 cannot override a hard exclusion. Record hard-excluded books in EXCLUDED_BOOKS.md, not in either scored book list, and do not assign them new scores or counterfactual veto scores.

## Veto rules - a book is excluded regardless of score when one of these is present at a serious level:
1. Epistemic failure - The central argument depends on material factual errors, distorted sources, invalid causal inference, concealed uncertainty, or obsolete information presented as current. 
2. Ideological capture - The argument depends on political, religious, national, cultural, or economic allegiance rather than evidence. Counterexamples are ignored, caricatured, or explained away asymmetrically. 
3. No transferable intellectual payload - After removing names, dates, personalities, and local detail, little remains in the form of mechanisms, constraints, models, heuristics, or generalizable lessons. Exceptions are mathematical, science or high-tech biographies or memoires like Skunk Works, "The man from the future" about von Neumann, "The man who solved the market" about Simons, "Never at rest" about Newton, etc...
4. Hype or ephemerality dependence - The book’s value depends primarily on a passing controversy, fashionable terminology, launch publicity, or premature claims of revolutionary importance. Educated speculative prediction is allowed if stated in professional substantiated balanced argumentative way with elaborate guardrails.
5. Religious doctrine or ideological extremism. Veto scripture, devotional or proselytizing works, and works whose central purpose is to establish religious doctrine as authoritative truth. Also veto explicit advocacy of ideological extremism, supported by specific evidence such as dehumanization, persecution, violent domination or suppression of dissent. A manifesto label, advocacy, activism, political position, technological vision, institutional criticism or proposed reform is not sufficient. Historical or analytical discussion of religion or extremist movements is not itself advocacy.
6. Partisan mobilization. Veto works whose dominant purpose is to recruit, rally, or reinforce allegiance to a political leader, party, or movement, or to supply campaign attacks against opponents. Establish this purpose from the introduction, representative passages, and stated aims. Apply the rule equally across political positions. A policy conclusion, institutional criticism, or reform proposal alone is insufficient.

### Assessing information density and length justification

Judge useful learning per unit of reading, not brevity, jargon, difficulty, or the number of headline theses. Count distinct explanatory facts, mechanisms, distinctions, evidence and illuminating cases. A new example of an existing framework earns credit when it adds historical knowledge, a different constraint, a comparison, a boundary condition or a useful application. Repetition is a deduction only when it adds little understanding. Do not treat a repeated framework as proof of padding, or a short book as proof of density.

Use the same anchored 0–10 attainment scale across topics: 9–10 sustained high learning yield with little removable material; 7–8 substantial learning with some recap or lower-yield stretches; 5–6 useful content mixed with substantial low-yield explanation or repetition; 3–4 a small payload spread over much of the book; 0–2 very little educational content for its length. Half-points may express an intermediate judgment, not measurement precision. Assess the whole book rather than only its strongest section. Distinguish educational examples from anecdotes that only restate the claim. Keep factual reliability under E, causal depth under M, clarity under N and topic preference under F; do not deduct the same concern again under D without a separate density reason.

Record the evidence inspected (full text, named sample chapters, contents, independent review, or reader report), examples of added learning and any identified low-yield material. Contents and publisher blurbs alone cannot establish paragraph-level density. Use high confidence only for broad direct text coverage; samples and corroborating reviews usually support moderate confidence, and description-only estimates are low confidence. Personal reading reports are relevant evidence but not objective measurements. The 0–10 ratings are judgments, not a ratio scale: 8 is not twice the information of 4. Retain prior estimates outside the reviewed set and label them as not yet recalibrated; do not claim a catalog-wide density audit from a few examples.

### Scoring calculation

Each maximum above is the maximum number of points that criterion can contribute. First assess the criterion's attainment as linear points `x` from 0 to its own maximum `m`.

- Apply a quadratic profile **only** to evidence and inferential integrity (`m = 20`), systems integration (`m = 17`), and information density and length justification (`m = 10`): `points = m × (x / m)^2 = x^2 / m`.
- All seven other positive criteria remain linear: `points = x`. Mechanistic and causal depth has a maximum of **12** and is **not quadratic**.
- Use the criterion's own maximum as the denominator, not a common 10-point scale. Zero attainment gives 0 points; full attainment gives the full maximum. Half attainment gives one quarter of the maximum on a quadratic criterion.
- Sum the ten resulting contributions, then apply rules 11–15 separately: `total = sum(points) + T11 + T12 + H13 + P14 + O15`. Each thematic deduction is 0 or −10; both can apply, for a combined −20. The positive maxima sum to 100. Do not square the total or any deduction.
- For rule 13, use the end of the central historical period: `H13 = -min(12, max(0, review_year - period_end_year) / 25)`. Use fractional points, not completed 25-year blocks. A timeless argument or broad cross-period account with no single central period receives 0. Publication age is not a substitute for topic age. Record the assessed endpoint and its confidence.
- For rule 14, use original publication age: `P14 = -min(10, max(0, review_year - original_year) / 10)`. Use fractional points, capped at a maximum deduction of 10 points. Reprints, translations and revised editions do not reset the original year. For ancient works, use an explicitly recorded approximate composition or compilation year and its confidence. For a nominated series, use its first volume's original year.
- For rule 15, use one matching Amazon product's rating aggregate, without adding counts across editions or marketplaces: fewer than 10 → −20; 10–99 → −10; 100–999 → −5; at least 1,000 → 0. These bands are mutually exclusive. Verify the title and author, distinguish total ratings from written reviews, and record the marketplace, product identifier, URL, retrieval date and any snapshot limitation. An unavailable count is unknown, not zero; after retrieval attempts, apply the specified fallback of no obscurity deduction and label that fallback.
- Do not output provisional range, but single numerical score per book. Use prescribed fallbacks if needed.
- Retain unrounded values for calculation, sorting and verdict thresholds. Display criterion contributions and totals to two decimal places. A displayed total can differ slightly from the sum of displayed components because components are rounded separately.
- When migrating an earlier linear assessment with an old maximum `m_old`, preserve its assessed attainment unless new evidence warrants a change: `x_new = m_new × (x_old / m_old)`. Then apply the current profile once. Thus an old systems-integration score `S` out of 12 becomes `17 × (S / 12)^2`, and an old causal-depth score `M` out of 17 becomes `12 × (M / 17)`. Keep the underlying linear assessment and the final contribution separate so a later update cannot apply the quadratic curve twice.

Use criterion names as the identity when an order or weight changes. In existing ledgers, `S` means systems integration and `M` means mechanistic and causal depth; do not swap their meanings. A scoring-profile change alone does not change a hard exclusion, veto, thematic classification, or confidence in the book-level evidence. Preserve historical analyses and record the revised breakdown for every current recommended and vetoed entry; vetoed totals remain counterfactual.
