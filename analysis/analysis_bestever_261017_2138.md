## Method and score key

Fiction is removed before assessment. Every identified nonfiction work then passes through V1 epistemic failure, V2 ideological capture, V3 no transferable payload, and V4 dependence on hype or passing controversy. A veto needs a serious central defect; topic dislike, political identity, popularity and a low score are not substitutes. Scientific and technical biographies retain the specified V3 exception. Describing extremism does not itself make a work extremist.

| Code | Criterion | Maximum |
|---|---|---:|
| E | Evidence and inferential integrity | 20 |
| M | Mechanistic and causal depth | 17 |
| U | Transferable strategic utility | 15 |
| S | Systems integration | 12 |
| D | Information density and length justification | 10 |
| R | Durability and resistance to hype | 9 |
| F | Direct domain fit | 7 |
| A | Expert authority and intellectual standing | 5 |
| N | Narrative architecture and clarity | 3 |
| P | Authorial precision and finesse | 2 |
| T | Predominant listed negative theme | 0 or -10, once |

**Total = E + M + U + S + D + R + F + A + N + P + T.** Points are weighted directly; there is no second weighting or rescaling. T is not multiplied when several unwanted themes overlap. An American author, a foreign setting, a sporting subject or self-help alone does not automatically trigger T. Domain fit and thematic deduction are separate criteria specified by the prompt.

Verdicts: **85–100 Recommend; 75–84 Consider; 65–74 Selective; 50–64 Low priority; below 50 Poor fit.** A veto overrides these bands. Counterfactual scores suspend only the veto gate and retain all identified defects. They do not make vetoed books recommended. Integer precision makes the arithmetic auditable; it does not make small score differences objective measurements.

Each book has a full vector, a stated theme and a reasoned assessment. These are desk assessments using the cited material and relevant prior analyses, not claims to have newly read 143 complete books. **M/L** mean moderate/low score confidence. Counterfactual scores all have low confidence; veto confidence is stated separately. Conditional identities are marked **†**. Uncertain nonfiction remains scored.

Reader ratings are separate from quality scores. **GR** is Goodreads, on a five-point scale; the following number is the rating count, rounded in the compact table. Newly retrieved values are indexed snapshots accessed on 2026-10-17, not guaranteed live totals. **p** marks a same-day snapshot carried from a cited prior report. Edition groupings and crawl dates can differ. No cross-platform average is invented; no Book Marks critic score is presented as a reader rating. **NR** means no reliable snapshot was recovered. How to Kid for Adults is the sole ranked NR entry after targeted searches.

Book links use Amazon.de edition locators derived from checked ISBNs or retained edition records. They are not confirmations of Amazon stock, price or delivery. Paperback is the default. **HC** marks the verified hardcover fallback for Catch the Devil and End of Days; no current paperback was established. **search** marks the ISBN search fallback for How to Kid for Adults, whose Amazon product identifier was not verified. First year refers to the work, not the linked reprint. Ancient works use approximate composition ranges.

Because it claims an autobiographical basis, it receives a reliability assessment and V1 here. **Bad Wisdom** and **The Prophet** are classified as fiction by the checked catalogue/publisher and are excluded before scoring.
