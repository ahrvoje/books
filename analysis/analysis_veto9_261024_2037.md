# V9 foundations review — 24 October 2026

Review started at 20:37 Europe/Warsaw.

9. Highly disputed foundations. Veto when specific, well-supported specialist criticism establishes material defects in the evidence or reasoning essential to the book’s central thesis, such that correcting them requires abandoning or substantially rebuilding that thesis. Assess the strongest available defenses and contrary evidence; require high confidence. Disagreement, author reputation, isolated errors, disputed scope, and openly acknowledged speculation are insufficient.

## Result and scope

Counts remain 5,407 recommended, 602 vetoed and 2,653 hard-excluded works. Scores, source IDs, rating and page metadata are unchanged. Earlier analyses remain history.

The earlier conversational classification was a list of disputes, not evidence that each book meets V9. In particular, *A New Kind of Science*, *Built to Last* and *David and Goliath* were candidates, not established vetoes. Applying the exact high-confidence rule does not warrant moving them on the reviewed evidence. This is not a claim that their central theories are true or well established.

The current snapshot contains 6,009 scored records. Their recorded reasons and criterion notes were screened for explicit failure/fabrication leads. The [screening manifest](../data/veto9_261024/screening-flags.json) contains 43 additional matches beyond the 52 disputed recommendations. Matches include books discussing failure or debunking, not necessarily suffering from it. Existing exclusions were kept outside scoring and V9 adjudication. The focused review below covers the 52 disputed recommendations plus Dingle; it is not a full-text audit of all 6,009 books.

A retained book’s confidence refers to this bounded decision, not confidence that its thesis is correct. Missing proof of a veto is not proof of sound foundations. Low-confidence unresolved cases stay scored under the saved rules. Existing V1–V8 vetoes remain in force. Other matching records receive no new V9 finding from a keyword match alone. The inherited scores and density estimates have not been recalibrated.
