# Revised V9 application — 24 October 2026, 20:50 Europe/Warsaw

9. Highly disputed foundations. Veto books whose central thesis or recurring explanatory narrative relies on speculative assumptions, weak causal inference, or contested philosophical premises challenged by substantial, reasoned specialist criticism. This includes sweeping historical or evolutionary explanations built from unsupported reconstructions or selected anecdotes. Require high confidence that the criticism concerns these foundations, not merely details, scope, or implications. Conclusive refutation is unnecessary; acknowledged uncertainty and sound supporting material do not exempt the central argument. Consider substantive defenses.

## Outcome and interpretation

Nine recommendations receive V9 with high confidence: *Sapiens* plus the eight titles marked high confidence in the preceding candidate list. The change to Sapiens is a change in the rule’s coverage, supported by the central narrative criticism; it is not new proof that the entire book is false. The earlier V9 required a demonstrated defect forcing abandonment or reconstruction. The revised V9 instead excludes substantially disputed foundations and explicitly covers recurring explanatory narratives. High confidence refers to matching this selection rule, not a claim of scholarly consensus, conclusive refutation, or falsity of every supporting fact.

The criticism, central dependence and substantive defense are recorded separately below. Sound exposition and admitted uncertainty no longer exempt the central argument. This explains the changes from [the initial V9 review](analysis_veto9_261024_2037.md), which remains historical. Earlier decisions releasing other gates, including V4 for The Fourth Turning, are not retrospectively reversed. V9 supplies the new independent ground.
