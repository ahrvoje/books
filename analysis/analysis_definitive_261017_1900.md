## Revised rules and scope

The thematic deduction is **0 or -10, applied once**. The listed themes now explicitly include **American tragedy**. For this revision, that means a predominant narrative of American disaster, traumatic loss or fatal institutional failure. The location can be outside the United States. American authorship, an American participant, or a difficult financial event alone does not establish this classification. This is a reader-preference judgment, not a claim about factual quality.

The ten positive criteria retain their prior values where the evidence has not changed. A new theme deduction does not create a veto. Counterfactual veto scores likewise retain the identified defects; only the veto gate is suspended for that calculation.

The user confirmed that the hard exclusion means **fiction**, correcting the accidental word “non-fiction” in AGENTS.md. Fiction is discarded before scoring and is not entered in VETOED_BOOKS.md. The two removed novels are recorded below without new scores. Historical primary sources about myths and poetic conventions are not automatically novels: the existing limited-use assessments of The Prose Edda and The Book of Invasions remain, with their low confidence and explicit limits. This is a genre distinction, not acceptance of their stories as factual history.

1. Use only the recovered thread to nominate titles. External sources check identity, subject, editions, reception, and factual concerns; they never add candidates.
2. After the fiction exclusion, apply the four vetoes only at a serious level: **V1**, central factual/inferential failure; **V2**, allegiance overriding evidence; **V3**, no transferable intellectual content, subject to the stated science/technology biography exceptions; **V4**, dependence on hype or a passing event. A political subject is not itself V2. A book outside the preferred topics is not itself V3.
3. Score all eligible, non-vetoed candidates. Do not introduce a minimum score, a topic-based admission gate, or an unscored “hold.” If the evidence is incomplete, give a provisional score and state its confidence.
4. Add the ten weighted criterion points, then subtract **10 once** if the specified negative themes predominate. Do not stack deductions for overlapping themes. The point maxima sum to 100.
5. Sort by total, descending. Equal totals use the title alphabetically as a stable tie-break, with no claim of a meaningful difference. Scores separated by a few points are not measured quality differences.
6. Reassess existing books using the accumulated evidence and the current rubric. Reconsider vetoes, every criterion, thematic deductions, verdicts, and confidence. A prior score is a judgment to review, not independent evidence; repeated mentions earn no quality bonus. Preserve the prior analysis and update each existing RECOMMENDED_BOOKS.md row without duplication.

The weights are unchanged: evidence **20**; mechanism **17**; transferable utility **15**; systems integration **12**; density **10**; durability **9**; direct domain fit **7**; authority **5**; narrative clarity **3**; prose precision **2**. A high total can coexist with an unwanted subject because the rubric gives strong weight to quality and transferable mechanisms. The verdict states the personal reading recommendation separately.

All score choices and statements about transfer beyond the book's immediate subject are this analysis's judgments. Publisher descriptions verify subject and format, not the validity of every causal claim. Reader stars and Reddit votes do not enter the score.

## Individual assessments

### 9. The Making of the Atomic Bomb — 88/100

**Verdict:** Recommend. **Veto decision:** none established. **Confidence:** moderate in the reading-priority score; high in source occurrence and selected paperback identity.

The strongest connection here is between scientific discovery, engineering feasibility, coordinated research, industrial effort, and military power. This provides more relevant value than its elementary physics explanations, which will add little for this reader. Rudolf Peierls's review supports the broad scientific and historical account while identifying minor explanatory errors and offering a different account of refugee scientists' involvement. These specific limits reduce evidence and causal-depth points without establishing a serious central failure. No thematic deduction applies: the programme is US-led, but the scientific origins, rival programmes, and strategic influence are international. [Rudolf Peierls, review](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1987/11/05/making-it/); [publisher and edition](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Making-of-the-Atomic-Bomb/Richard-Rhodes/9781451677614).
