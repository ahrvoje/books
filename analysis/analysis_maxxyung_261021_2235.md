## Retained vetoes

- **Good To Great** — Jim Collins: Retain V1 at moderate confidence: the documented selection of transition dates, missing risk adjustment and weak out-of-period checks undermine the advertised discovery of portable success causes. Collins's acknowledgment of correlation and the framework's practical interest are considered. Later company failures alone are not the reason.
- **The Four Agreements** — Don Miguel Ruiz: Retain V5: the work teaches an inherited Toltec spiritual framework and liberation through its agreements as authoritative guidance. This is more than a secular argument that happens to use religious examples; its teaching purpose is central.
- **The Future Is Faster Than You Think** — Peter H. Diamandis; Steven Kotler: Retain V4: the book's published flying-car extract moves from demonstrations and company targets to declaring the transition already here and infrastructure imminent. Together with its decade-wide transformation promise, this shows premature importance claims rather than sufficiently guarded scenario analysis.
- **Why We Sleep** — Matthew Walker: Retain V1: the detailed prior critique and author response leave material health-risk overstatements and evidential handling unresolved. The decision is about those central claims, not the importance of sleep itself.
- **The Body Keeps the Score** — Bessel van der Kolk: Retain V1 at moderate confidence: the specialist claim audit identifies unsupported central claims about trauma’s effects on brain function and the special efficacy of body-based treatments. This does not imply that trauma is unreal or that all therapies discussed lack evidence; the veto is tied to the book’s overgeneralized explanatory and treatment claims.
