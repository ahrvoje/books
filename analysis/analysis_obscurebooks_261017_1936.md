## Method and evidence limits

1. Use the post and its comments as the only source of candidates. Ignore suggested posts and navigation below the thread. General praise of an author's entire output does not nominate additional titles.
2. Resolve spelling, abbreviated titles, first publication and genre using bibliographic records. A descriptive reference can identify a book; a vague biography cannot. Record unresolved identity instead of silently inventing one.
3. Apply fiction exclusion first. Then test the four serious vetoes. Poor topic fit, a political viewpoint, unfamiliarity and low reader ratings are not vetoes.
4. Reassess all seven overlaps with source 2, including the veto gate and every criterion. Consult both its [latest analysis](analysis_top3alltime_261017_1900.md) and [prior detailed assessment](analysis_top3alltime_261017_1842.md). Do not average scores or treat repeated mentions as independent evidence.
5. Score all remaining works, including low-fit and uncertain ones. Apply one thematic deduction at most. Retain the full flawed assessment when calculating a vetoed book's counterfactual score.
6. Generate the analysis and current book-list entries from the same score records; verify arithmetic, membership, source numbers and duplicate handling.

The research combines publisher descriptions and excerpts, bibliographic records, reader-rating pages, accessible reviews, targeted specialist criticism and background knowledge. It is **not a new cover-to-cover reading of 153 books or an audit of every claim**. Catalogue descriptions establish subject and format better than factual reliability. Numerical assessments of whole-book evidence, density and prose are provisional where direct evidence is thin. **M = moderate confidence; L = low confidence.** No whole-book score here is assigned high confidence. Veto confidence is separate.

Reader approval is not evidence of causal validity. Ratings below use **Goodreads only**, with the observed rating count, rather than an invented average across Book Marks, LibraryThing, StoryGraph and Amazon. They were retrieved on 17 October 2026 from pages or indexed snapshots whose crawl dates differ; they are not synchronized live counts. **NR** means no usable rating was retrieved. Book Marks measures critics' reception, which should not be silently pooled with reader stars. Small samples receive no quality bonus.

## Scoring contract

| Criterion | Maximum | What earns credit |
|---|---:|---|
| E — evidence and inference | 20 | Reliable sources; sound claims; explicit limits |
| M — mechanisms and causes | 17 | Constraints, incentives, feedback and failure modes |
| U — transferable utility | 15 | Reusable models, methods and decisions |
| S — systems integration | 12 | Coherent links across relevant layers |
| D — density and justified length | 10 | Reading cost supported by useful content |
| R — durability | 9 | Value beyond a news cycle or fashion |
| F — direct domain fit | 7 | Your stated scientific, industrial and strategic interests |
| A — authority | 5 | Expertise and serious specialist engagement |
| N — narrative structure | 3 | Clear, cumulative and memorable organization |
| P — precision | 2 | Controlled, exact prose and concepts |
| T — thematic deduction | 0 or −10 | One deduction when a listed negative theme predominates |

**Total = E + M + U + S + D + R + F + A + N + P + T.** The positive maxima sum to 100. Scores are already weighted points; there is no second weighting or normalization. Verdict bands: **85–100 Recommend; 75–84 Consider; 65–74 Selective; 50–64 Low priority; below 50 Poor fit.** A veto overrides all bands. “Retained” means included under the rules, not necessarily worth your reading time.

T concerns predominant content, not author nationality or the presence of one unwanted topic. Sports and religion are not additional automatic deductions under rule 11; poor fit instead affects F and relevant utility. Ancient urban history is not exempt from the urban-history deduction. International trade or navigation is not automatically single-nation history. A national strategic history can earn strong mechanism points and still receive T. Science and high-tech biographies can pass V3 without escaping other vetoes or thematic deductions.
