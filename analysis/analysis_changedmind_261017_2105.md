## Decision rule

Fiction is removed first. V1 = serious failure of evidence or inference; V2 = serious dependence on ideological allegiance; V3 = too little transferable intellectual content; V4 = serious dependence on hype or a passing controversy. Subject mismatch and low scores do not add exclusion rules. A disputed claim or explicit normative position does not automatically establish a veto.

The ten components are weighted points, not ten separate percentages:

| Code | Criterion | Maximum |
|---|---|---:|
| E | Evidence and inferential integrity | 20 |
| M | Mechanistic and causal depth | 17 |
| U | Transferable strategic utility | 15 |
| S | Systems integration | 12 |
| D | Information density and length justification | 10 |
| R | Durability and resistance to hype | 9 |
| F | Direct domain fit | 7 |
| A | Expert authority and intellectual standing | 5 |
| N | Narrative architecture and clarity | 3 |
| P | Authorial precision and finesse | 2 |

**Total = E+M+U+S+D+R+F+A+N+P+T.** T is **0 or -10 once**, not once per unwanted theme. It applies only when a listed theme predominates. American authorship alone, the presence of animals, gender as a subject, and personal life in one country are not automatic deductions. Philosophy is judged for argumentative integrity; it is not required to imitate experimental science. Technical familiarity reduces utility where appropriate.

Verdict bands match the recent repository analyses: **85–100 Recommend; 75–84 Consider; 65–74 Selective; 50–64 Low priority; below 50 Poor fit.** These are reading-priority judgments, not measured probabilities. A one-point gap is not a robust empirical distinction. Veto overrides every band. Counterfactual scores suspend only the veto gate and retain all identified defects; their score confidence is low.
