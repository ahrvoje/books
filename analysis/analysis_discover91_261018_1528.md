## Scoring convention

E = evidence and inferential integrity /20; S = systems integration /17; U = transferable strategic utility /15; M = mechanistic and causal depth /12; D = information density and length justification /10; R = durability /9; F = direct domain fit /7; A = expert authority /5; N = narrative architecture and clarity /3; P = authorial precision /2.

Only **E, S and D** use `x²/m`. All other contributions equal raw attainment x. T is either 0 or −10, applied once. Raw assessments and contributions are stored separately. Decimal display is to two places; calculations, sorting and thresholds use unrounded values.

## Source 45 — What Engineers Know and How They Know It

**Walter G. Vincenti, 1990. Subtitle: Analytical Studies from Aeronautical History. 91.21 — Recommend; moderate score confidence.**

Its strongest use for this reader is to show how reliable engineering knowledge emerges when theory, experiment, manufacturing and users disagree. Requirements themselves can be discoveries. In [Ian Alexander's review](https://www.scenarioplus.org.uk/reviews/vincenti.htm), the aircraft handling cases show why correct models and sensible initial specifications can still fail to capture practical needs. This is directly relevant to model validation and technical development.

**Strongest objection:** the five aeronautical cases do not establish a complete theory of engineering. [Stacey and colleagues, Design Science, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/dsj.2025.9) retain Vincenti as a foundational starting point but identify method knowledge as missing from his classification. This limits the final taxonomy; it does not, on the evidence inspected, invalidate the historical cases. Reader reviews also report repetition and excessive detail. D=9 is a judgment that most detail earns its place for this technically trained reader, while N=2 recognizes the demanding structure.

**Hard exclusions:** nonfiction; rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes, high confidence: the dominant purpose is to explain the development and character of engineering knowledge, through historical cases and a concluding argument. It is not a course sequence, design handbook or procedural training text. The 2025 paper explicitly identifies explanation to non-engineers as its purpose. Technical material is evidence for the history.

**Vetoes:** V1 not established; the incomplete taxonomy reduces the assessment. V2 and V5 not established: the inspected argument does not require ideological allegiance or activism. V3 does not apply because the cases yield reusable models of learning, specification and design. V4 does not apply to this mature historical argument. Confidence in the absence of an established veto: moderate.

## Source 46 — Inside the Black Box

**Nathan Rosenberg, 1982. Subtitle: Technology and Economics. 91.30 — Recommend; moderate score confidence.**

This is the strongest match for understanding how technical constraints enter economic explanations. The useful questions concern complementary technologies, learning during use, uncertain adoption decisions, international transfer, and feedback from technology into science. The [author's preface extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/inside-the-black-box/preface/F955F0039140E9509FFB5C5DAEFA9BFB) states this explanatory purpose. These are tools for examining industrial growth, not a forecast of the next successful product.

[Jonathan Liebenau's contemporary review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228122662_Inside_the_Black_Box_Technology_and_Economics) praises its careful scrutiny of assumptions and its account of technological interdependence. The review text is signed **Jonathan Liebenau**; ResearchGate incorrectly labels its author as Rosenberg. [Joel Mokyr's retrospective](https://organizationsandmarkets.com/2015/09/07/mokyr-on-rosenberg/) supports the continuing value of the science–technology feedback argument and Rosenberg's concise analytical prose. It is an admiring obituary, not an independent test of every claim.

**Strongest objection:** this is a collection of essays, with uneven continuity and dated contemporary examples. The early-1980s electronics and competition discussions must be read as historical analysis. R=9 rewards the durable mechanisms, not the currency of every sector claim; N=2 reflects the collection's structure. E=18 leaves room for selective examples and limits to empirical generalization. No extrapolation to today's chip market is credited.

**Hard exclusions:** nonfiction, so rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes with moderate confidence. Its original audience includes economists and economic historians, but its main form is explanatory historical argument across technologies and industries. It does not teach an econometric method, supply technical reference specifications, or provide a training sequence. Scholarly audience alone is not decisive under the rule; the content and purpose are. Some chapters are jointly written with David C. Mowery or W. Edward Steinmueller; the book is catalogued under Nathan Rosenberg.

**Vetoes:** no established V1, V2, V4 or V5; V3 does not apply because mechanisms dominate. In particular, the chapter on Marx is not evidence by itself of ideological capture. The [chapter extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/inside-the-black-box/marx-as-a-student-of-technology/E25DA3DCEE1F7B749B433F8589709E71) explicitly separates the value of Marx's questions from whether his predictions were correct. It does not demand political adherence. Veto-assessment confidence: moderate.

## Source 47 — Fighting the Great War at Sea

**Norman Friedman, 2014. Subtitle: Strategy, Tactics and Technology. 91.30 — Recommend; moderate score confidence.**

This connects hardware to the system that makes it useful: finance, production, trade, geography, logistics, intelligence, doctrine and operational limits. [A. A. Nofi's review](https://lvm.strategypage.com/bookreviews/1321) identifies explicit treatment of the financial and industrial foundations of fleets. This supports full systems-integration credit; the score does not rest on the range of ship types alone.

[Mark Lardas's review](https://navyhistory.org/2014/12/book-review-fighting-the-war-at-sea-strategy-tactics/) highlights the roles of logistics, personnel and communications, while noting limited attention to several other navies. The transferable lesson is to test a force's supporting system and actual operating constraints, rather than infer capability from equipment specifications. That application beyond naval history is my inference.

**Strongest objection:** [Joseph Moretz's critical review](https://navyhistory.org/2020/08/fighting-the-great-war-at-sea-strategy-tactics/) finds that fact and authorial inference are not always clearly separated and warns about treating Maurice Hankey's memoirs as neutral evidence. The British–German focus also limits coverage. These are real E and M constraints. They do not demonstrate that the central integration of technology and strategy fails, so V1 is not established.

**Hard exclusions:** nonfiction; rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes, high confidence. The dominant structure explains a war and the interaction of capabilities, resources and decisions. This is not a ship-design reference, weapons manual or guide to performing naval operations. Moretz expressly discusses its value for general readers as well as naval professionals. Specialist detail alone is not the exclusion test.

**Vetoes:** V1 concerns are retained in scoring. V2 and V5 are not established by the inspected evidence. V3 does not apply: the book explains mechanisms, constraints and mistaken expectations. V4 does not apply to its established historical subject. Veto-assessment confidence: moderate.

## Source 48 — The Invention That Changed the World

[Witmer's review](https://historynet.com/book-review-the-invention-that-changed-the-world-robert-buderi-ww2/) supports its accessible account of moving radar from laboratories into wartime use. **Hard exclusions:** nonfiction and a history for general readers, not instructional engineering; both pass, high confidence. **Vetoes:** none established, moderate confidence. ## Source 49 — Inventing Accuracy

**Donald MacKenzie, 1990. Subtitle: A Historical Sociology of Nuclear Missile Guidance. Veto V5; moderate veto confidence. Counterfactual 87.85; low score confidence.**

The strongest reason to retain it intellectually is its detailed account of how institutions, tests and engineering choices create military capability. But the user's V5 is a separate gate. [Paul K. Couchman's 1993 review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261683308_Inventing_Accuracy_A_Historical_Sociology_of_Nuclear_Missile_Guidance_by_Donald_MacKenzie_MIT_Press_Cambridge_MA_1990_pp_xiii_464_US2995_ISBN_0-262-13258-3) identifies challenging the continued existence and development of nuclear weapons as one of the book's three purposes, and discusses its disarmament epilogue. This is stronger evidence of an activist purpose than merely mentioning arms control.

**V5 applies, moderate confidence.** The contrary interpretation is that a short normative conclusion is incidental to a substantial history. The review's description of intervention as a book-level purpose tips the judgment toward veto under this repository's strict rule. The full epilogue was not inspected, so confidence is not high. The veto is about the work's stated purpose, not MacKenzie's affiliation, and does not erase its research value.

**Hard exclusions pass first:** historical nonfiction; the argument explains technological development across engineering, sociology and national security rather than training missile specialists. Rule-2 confidence is moderate. V1 and V2 are not separately established at a serious level. V3 and V4 do not apply.

[William Thomas's historiographical critique](https://etherwave.wordpress.com/2009/08/14/normative-historiography-and-the-gallery-of-practices/) also raises the problem of treating intertwined causes as grounds for dissolving useful analytical distinctions. Thomas quotes David Edgerton's 1993 review; the original review was not read in full. This criticism limits E and M; it is not treated as proof that the empirical history is false.

The counterfactual suspends **only V5** and retains these limitations.
