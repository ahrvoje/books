### Description

An Important Work, Colour Plates, First Edition, Illustrated, Publishers' Original Binding, Signed

The first American edition of this fantastic work by Fridtjof Nansen. Complete in two volumes.
Signed to a loose blank page to the first volume "with kindest regards, Fridtjof Nansen."
Narrating of the history of the famous Fram expedition led by Dr Nansen from 1893 to 1896, in which he tried to reach the geographical North Pole by harnessing the north-east current of the Arctic Ocean. 
Even though the mission was discouraged by other explorers, Nansen took the Fram with his companion Hjalmas Johansen to the eastern Arctic Ocean, to the New Siberian Islands, and waited for the water to carry it towards the pole. After eighteen months of impatient and slow speed, they both left the ship and carried on the expedition with a team of Samoyed dogs and sledges to reach the Pole. 
Although they did not reach it, they did reach the Farthest North latitude of 86°13.6′N. The Fram finally emerged in the North Atlantic Ocean.
With about one hundred and twenty full page and various in text illustrations, sixteen coloured plates in facsimile from Nansen's own sketches, etched portrait, photogravures, and four loose maps to the front pastedown's pocket to volume one. Collated, complete.
With an Appendix by Otto Sverdrup, Captain of the Fram.

Nansen, one of the most significant figures in the history of Arctic exploration, was originally a naturalist. He decided in 1888 to attempt the crossing of Greenland, and with five companions, including Otto Sverdrup, set off in June of that year. The crossing, by small boat, ski and sledge, achieved many important scientific objectives, including meteorological observations, as well as illustrating the ideal methods of Arctic travel. His most important achievements were on the Fram Expedition of 1893-96. He established the nature and drift of the Arctic Ice-pack, having deliberately allowed the ship, built to his own design, to be frozen into the ice. # Farthest North

He built a ship the ice could not crush, froze her in on purpose, and drifted across the polar ocean for three years - then walked away from her towards the Pole with one man and twenty-eight dogs.

The journey
    1893-1896
Where
    The Arctic and the North
Author
    Fridtjof Nansen
Written
    Written from the ship's and the sledge journals
Language

## The journey

Why they went
    Science — Collecting, measuring, describing - natural history, geology, ethnography.
Survey — Charting and mapping, usually for a state, a navy or a company.
Every man who sailed came back.

## What we read now

A contemporary English translation, free, in two substantial volumes.

The science sections have dated and the narrative has not. The account of the winter in the hut - two men, one lamp, bear grease, and nothing whatever to do for nine months - is one of the strangest things in this literature.

How it reaches us

Written
    Written from the ship's and the sledge journals
First printed
    1897
First in English
    1897
See it
    Yes

The contemporary English translation appeared with the Norwegian and is out of copyright and freely available in two volumes. There is no modern scholarly edition in general circulation.

Published in Norwegian as *Fram over Polhavet* in 1897 and in English the same year - a rare case on this wall of a book reaching English immediately rather than decades later, because the expedition was an international sensation and the publishers were waiting.

## What it cost

Nothing in lives, which is why it is the outlier.
