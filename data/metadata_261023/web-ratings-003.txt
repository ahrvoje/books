Jun 22 2023 • 5 mins

The statement "Show me who you run with, and I will show you your life" and the idea of the "sum of the five people you hang around most" both highlight the influence that the people we surround ourselves with can have on our lives.

The people we spend the most time with can greatly impact our thoughts, beliefs, attitudes, behaviors, and overall life trajectory. This is because humans are inherently social beings, and we tend to absorb and align ourselves with the energy, values, and behaviors of those around us.

If we surround ourselves with positive, motivated, and growth-oriented individuals, their mindset and habits can inspire and uplift us. We are more likely to adopt their positive traits, engage in supportive conversations, and pursue personal development and success.

On the other hand, if we consistently spend time with negative, pessimistic, or toxic individuals, their attitudes and behaviors can seep into our own lives. We may find ourselves engaging in negative thinking, destructive habits, or being held back from reaching our full potential.

The notion of the "sum of the five people you hang around most" suggests that the combined influence of the closest individuals in our lives has a significant impact on who we become. It implies that the collective mindset, values, and behaviors of our core social circle play a crucial role in shaping our own attitudes, beliefs, and success.

Therefore, it becomes essential to choose our companions wisely and consciously surround ourselves with individuals who align with our aspirations and values. It doesn't mean that we need to sever ties with everyone who isn't on the same path as us, but rather to seek out and build relationships with people who inspire, support, and challenge us to grow.

By consciously selecting positive and uplifting individuals as our companions, we can create an environment that fosters personal growth, motivation, and success. These individuals can serve as role models, mentors, and sources of inspiration on our own journeys.

It's important to remember that while our environment and the people we associate with can have a significant impact on us, we still possess the power of choice and personal agency. We can actively choose to cultivate positive influences, adopt empowering beliefs, and strive for personal growth, regardless of the circumstances or people around us.

Ultimately, by surrounding ourselves with individuals who embody the qualities and values we aspire to, we can create a supportive and enriching environment that propels us towards our goals and helps us live a more fulfilling and successful life.

# If It Isn’t Singing, Take It Out: Rich Ferguson on poetry, rhythm, and the embodied word

The Sum of All Wisdom: Conversations on Music, Makers, Meaning & More

Where is a poem?

For poet, drummer, novelist, and performer Rich Ferguson, poetry lives in the breath, the body, and the beat. It moves through voice and gesture, enters into conversation with music and silence, and gathers new meaning from the people and places surrounding it.

In this conversation, Rich joins Scott Catey to explore the deep musicality of language and the entwining of music and poetry. He explains how cadence becomes an editing tool, why memorizing a poem is a physical process, and how years of preparation make spontaneity possible. As Rich puts it: if a passage doesn’t sing, it doesn’t belong in the poem.

They also discuss the continuing legacy of Beat poetry and its power to awaken attention. At a time when art is increasingly encountered through scrolling, fragments, and disposable moments, what can poetry and other artistic forms do to slow us down and return us to direct experience?

Rich describes Poet Film Stage, the multidisciplinary performance series he created with poet, playwright, and director Kathleen Florence. Combining poetry, theater, movement, music, and carefully placed silence, Poet Film Stage transforms a traditional reading into a collective journey. No single poem or performer is the destination; meaning emerges from the relationships among them.

The conversation culminates in Rich’s performance of “Live and Die in LA,” a propulsive spoken-word portrait of the city he once fled, but ultimately learned to love. Moving through music, violence, racial history, celebrity, incarceration, reinvention, and the accumulated ghosts of Los Angeles, the poem becomes both love letter and reckoning—a city carried in the blood and breath of the poet.

# Tools team

The tools team builds and manages the CI/CD pipeline. They are responsible for the infrastructure and tools that make up the pipeline. They create a tool that is used by the application and infrastructure teams in the organization. The organization needs to continuously mature its tools team, so that the tools team stays one step ahead of the maturing application and infrastructure teams.

The tools team must be skilled in building and integrating all parts of the CI/CD pipeline. This includes building source control repositories, workflow engines, build environments, testing frameworks, and artifact repositories. This team may choose to implement a tool such as Amazon CodeCatalyst, and AWS CodePipeline as well as Jenkins, GitHub, or other similar tools. Some organizations might call this a DevOps team, but AWS discourages this and instead encourages thinking of DevOps as the sum of the people, processes, and tools in software delivery.

The hosts added RAB Express condensed episodes in season ten. Mel and Moulz keep a rankings board on their website. They welcome listeners' input.

The podcast's rating system is intricate. The hosts rate each song on a scale of one to ten. They give albums three ratings. The takeaway rating is the percentage of songs that scored a seven or higher. The average rating is the sum of the scores divided by the highest possible score. The weighted rating is the sum of the takeaway rating and the average rating divided by two. The hosts then combine their weighted ratings and divide them by two for the definitive rating. There are special designations for high-ranking albums. Listeners learn which album they awarded with their top status before episode one.

The podcast hosts say debating music is what they enjoy most. Moulz calls the beat "bulky" on Kanye West's (Ye's) "Stronger," despite it having been remixed 75 times.
