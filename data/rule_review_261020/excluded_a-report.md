## Interpretation and changes

The revised H2 was checked against the current on-disk text, including its final restriction on explicit exam, instruction or reference purposes. A course origin, professional audience, technical difficulty, practical application or exercises do not alone decide exclusion. A substantive explanatory or narrative structure can qualify an expert work. Explicit procedure, reference and exam purposes still matter; an incidental anecdote does not override them.

Notable reversals include **Build**, **Computer Engineering**, **From Mathematics to Generic Programming**, **Grothendieck–Serre Correspondence**, **Noncommutative Geometry**, **Gaining Ground**, **Euler as Physicist**, **Mathematics and Its History**, **Galois' Dream**, and **Leçons de mathématiques d'aujourd'hui**. Their decisions identify the actual narrative or explanatory structure and retain limitations on score confidence. Several business, investment and reflective works also pass the narrower gate. **A Random Walk Down Wall Street** has a sustained market-history and economic argument; **Market Wizards** is a sequence of interviews and career narratives. Neither was admitted merely because broad practical advice is now allowed.

**Noncommutative Geometry** was checked again after the exact wording of the expert exception was highlighted. Connes's own introduction states a research-program purpose; it connects geometry, operator algebras and physics through cumulative explanation and substantial historical accounts. The preface and stated purpose do not establish exam preparation, operating instructions or a lookup-reference aim. Admission remains moderate confidence. This decision concerns Connes's original work, not Connes–Marcolli.

**A Pattern Language**, explicit certification guides, operational references and **Kill It with Fire** remain H2. For the last title, the modernization procedures and exercises remain the main purpose despite substantial case material. **Number Theory 1: Fermat's Dream** remains H2 with low confidence: available evidence does not resolve whether its explanatory development is sufficiently independent of course instruction. This is a recorded boundary, not a categorical rejection of advanced mathematics.

## Separate veto review

**Good to Great** passes H2 but receives V1. The Wake Forest account of Resnick and Smunt's analysis identifies transition-date data mining and inadequate risk and significance controls in the selection of great companies. These issues affect the central inference to transferable management causes. The decision also records Collins's matched comparisons and correlation caveat, and the possible usefulness of the framework. It does not rest on later company failures alone. **Built to Last** was examined separately: retrospective selection and halo concerns reduce evidence and causal attainment, but the specific serious V1 case was not established by transferring the other book's statistical critique.

**Karl Marx's Theory of History** is recommended after checking Cohen's distinction between interpretation and truth, his objections and later revisions. Defense of a theory alone was not treated as V5. **Man, Economy, and State** also remains eligible with low score confidence: Rothbard's apodictic method and direct methodological criticism lower evidence and causal scores. Austrian affiliation does not by itself establish V5. The original 1962 treatise was not conflated with *Power and Market* (1970).

## Dates, identity and source qualifications

Original dates were separated from translations, reprints and expanded editions. Material corrections or qualifications include Bacaër's French 2008 original, Connes's French 1990 original, Simon's German 2013 pricing book, Alexandrov's Russian 1956 survey, the 1968 Japanese original of *Galois' Dream*, and the November 2014 original of *From Mathematics to Generic Programming*.

- *Feynman's Thesis* uses the 1942 original research production, with an explicit note that the edited book appeared in 2005; the central research material extends through 1948. This applies the current original-production wording rather than silently treating the edited collection as a new argument.
- *Leçons de mathématiques d'aujourd'hui* is a generic series nomination. Bordeaux's 1996 collected lectures precede the 2000 Cassini volume. The record uses 1996 at moderate confidence, identifies the editorial identity and explains the later expanded collection.
- *Grothendieck–Serre Correspondence* uses the central 1955–1970 correspondence, with its 1984–1987 coda explicitly distinguished from the main period.
- *Computer Engineering* uses the authors' stated 1957–1977 case period.
- *Gaining Ground* uses an approximate deep-time endpoint of 300 million years ago. This reaches the H13 cap of −12. The year is an approximate geological boundary, not a precise calendar date.
- *Chasing the Rabbit* / *The High-Velocity Edge* is an edition/title identity to merge across batches. *Diaminds* has Roger L. Martin as coauthor; the historical input omitted him. These qualifications were raised with the integrating reviewer.

Every decision retains numeric source support. Broad compilation reports were not treated as evidence that every title occurs in every source. Catalog records with conflicting or implausible years were not allowed to replace the manually checked original year merely because the title matched.
