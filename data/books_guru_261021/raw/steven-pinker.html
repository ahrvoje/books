Steven Pinker Book Recommendations

Steven Pinker is an experimental psychologist and linguist. He is known for his advocacy of evolutionary psychology and the computational theory of mind, as well as his views on politics and rationalism. Pinker is a professor of psychology at Harvard University.

The Blind Watchmaker

4.5
2 005 ratings
Richard Dawkins
Recommended by: Steven Pinker, Jeff Bezos, Charlie Munger
Steven Pinker

Perhaps the best display of expository scientific prose of the twentieth century

source
In this insightful book, the author claims that evolution by natural selection explains the complicated design in the living being much better than the idea of an intelligent designer. He uses crystal-clear examples to show how very simple beginnings can indeed be enlarged and put together into very intricate structures by natural processes. The phrase blind watchmaker in the title is there to show that nature, which is seemingly purposeful, it works without foresight. The work refutes the notion of creationism and gives support to the theory of evolution as the true reasoning of diversity of life on Earth. It's a compelling read for anyone interested in understanding the mechanisms of evolution.

The Beginning of Infinity

4.6
1 654 ratings
David Deutsch
Recommended by: Steven Pinker, Mark Zuckerberg, Sam Altman
Steven Pinker

This 21st-century statement of the ideals of the Enlightenment offers fresh insight on a vast number of topics, including the workings of human cognition, the ways of science, and the drivers of progress

source
Unlock potentials for infinite progress, the only driver of which is human knowledge and creativity. This book further illustrates how through correct comprehension and application of science and technology, there is nothing impossible for mankind to accomplish. It runs the gamut of problems from physics and philosophy to the arts and history, showing how given enough knowledge, problems are solvable. The author challenges the concept that growth has finite boundaries and offers an optimistic view of the future where progress is infinite. It is an inspiring call to chase knowledge and innovation and to be shown endless opportunities lying ahead.

The Constitution of Knowledge

4.6
806 ratings
Jonathan Rauch
Recommended by: Steven Pinker
Steven Pinker

Vitally important book on truth in our time, The Constitution of Knowledge. Highly recommended

source
In this penetrating investigation, Jonathan Rauch looks at how society distinguishes truth from falsehood. He speaks to the "Constitution of Knowledge" and names it a set of norms and institutions which could be an operating system for society: those that guide our collective pursuit of truth. Rauch discusses the kind of system that is based on free speech, open debate, and empirical testing as now under threat in today's digital age. He brings out the key aspect regarding defending and reviving these principles to sustain healthy democracies, where a well-informed public can be made possible. This book is a compelling call to protect the integrity of our truth-seeking endeavors.

Whole Earth Discipline

4.5
257 ratings
Stewart Brand
Recommended by: Steven Pinker, Marc Andreessen
Steven Pinker

 Children of the 1970s will appreciate the title, an allusion to Brand’s groundbreaking “Whole Earth Catalog,” which merged technology with the counterculture and encouraged global consciousness with the breathtaking earthrise photograph on the cover

source
In "Whole Earth Discipline," Stewart Brand argues that people have to start changing their strategies in regard to environmental issues that take into account urbanization, genetically modified organisms, and nuclear power as solutions to the issue of climate change. He criticizes traditional ideologies of environmentalism to see science and technology as partners in achieving sustainability. The book is an entreaty for the readers to think critically about the ecological problems and appeals on adopting innovative approaches to saving our planet. Brand's insights offer a fresh perspective on how we can address the urgent environmental challenges facing us today.

A novel of ideas which traces the life road of the psychologist Cass Seltzer, who has grown famous thanks to his book which has advocated that there is no God, using thirty-six diverse arguments. The Plague Cycle

4.4
186 ratings
Charles Kenny
Recommended by: Steven Pinker
It is tracking infectious diseases from ancient times to modernity and their profound impact on human civilization. The book explains how plagues formed societies, economies, and governments, influencing from the increase of population to the rise and fall of empires. It also traces the evolution of the public health measures and medical breakthroughs, which have armed humanity to fight back those threats. Indicative of this constantly ongoing battle between man and the pathogen underlines the crucial aspect that preparedness, innovation, and global collaboration would play in deterring pandemics that might happen in the future. A compelling read that connects past challenges with current health crises, offering lessons for a healthier future.

Getting Better

141 ratings
Charles Kenny
Recommended by: Steven Pinker, Bill Gates
Steven Pinker

Kenny's book "Getting Better" was a major inspiration for my Enlightenment Now

source
For the last couple of centuries, explore the inspiring story of change in the overall global health, education, and quality of life. Despite all kinds of economic inequalities the world over, quality of life saw major improvements, which were not directly proportionate to the increase in income. This book is a cornerstone in depicting how innovations in medicine, technology, and policy have brought about longer lifespans of humans, less poverty, and much greater access to educational institutions across the human population. The book is an analytical eye-opener into how conflicts can be resolved using strategic thinking and game theory. How You Say It

4.2
159 ratings
Katherine D. Kinzler
Recommended by: Steven Pinker
Steven Pinker

A fascinating book on language and social relations by Katherine Kinzler

source
In "How You Say It," Katherine D. Kinzler reveals what an utterly dramatic impact language has on our existence. She shows how speaking affects the identity, relationships, and social status of an individual. In this book, Kinzler, through engrossing research and stories, shows how judgments based on language tend to crowd out the content of whatever is being said. This book calls upon the readers to turn their perception of language anew, a critical force that shapes our world. It's a thought-provoking read for anyone interested in the intersection of language, society, and psychology.
