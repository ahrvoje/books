Les Schwab was an American businessman who founded Les Schwab Tire Centers, a highly successful independent tire store chain in the western United States. Schwab came from humble beginnings and built his business from a single store in 1952 to hundreds of locations across multiple states, making Les Schwab one of the largest independent tire retailers in the country.

Les Schwab. I am pleased to announce that this year marks the 50th anniversary of the company he founded, Les Schwab Tires.

In 1952, Les bought a small tire store in the city of Prineville, deep in the heart of Central Oregon, for $3,500. From that simple investment, Les Schwab Tires has grown to become the nation’s leading independent tire business with over 330 stores throughout the West. Today, there is hardly a town in Oregon that does not fall under the shadow of a Les Schwab Tires sign.

His innovative business programs inspire a fierce loyalty that few other companies can match. The tenets for achievement are the same for all of his 7,000 employees: start at the bottom, work hard, and never rest on your laurels. The many profit sharing programs he pioneered ensure all employees have a stake in the success or failure of a store. To this end, the company returns over forty-nine percent of each store’s profits to its employees. As Les explained in his inspirational book, “Pride In Performance”, this return investment is “unselfish for good reasons.” --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## About the book

First published in 1986 and expanded by three chapters for the 1996 third printing shown here, Schwab's self-written autobiography traces his path into tire retail and sets out the operating policies he wanted the company to preserve: share store profits, pay for quality, promote from within, keep decisions close to customers, and resist complacency.

## Reading perspective

Schwab recounts building his tire-retailing company from the founder's chair. The memoir is organized around the operating policies he set at the level of the individual store.
