Announcing a new edition of a text on the growth of Western Civilization, which coordinates cultural and intellectual history with social, economic, and political developments.

A History of Civilization  
3rd Edition, 1967

by Crane Brinton, Harvard University; John B. Christopher, University of Rochester; and Robert Lee Wolff, Harvard University, two volumes, Vol. I, 672 pp., March 1967; Vol. II 720 pp., April 1967; each volume will be $9.95

Completely updated and revised, the 3rd Edition of A HISTORY OF CIVILIZATION unfolds a moving picture of the growth of Western Civilization, introducing non-European cultures as they impinged on European development. The authors discuss such subtle interactions as the effect of intellectual revolutions upon social and economic patterns, the influence of social attitudes in literature and music, and the reflection of religious convictions in political developments.

The new edition contains color tabs for each chapter, new illustrations, and maps have been redrawn and are in color. In Volume I the introduction to the Survey of Prehistoric Man has been revised and updated, and the sections on the Greek and Roman cultures has been completely rewritten and revised to provide more substantial integration. Volume II has been revised and updated with an extension of the theme of developing nationalism in the underdeveloped countries.

Volume I presents material from Prehistory to 1715 and Volume II covers the material to the present.

### Summary

A History of the Middle Ages is the amazing story of European man in transition. It is a dramatic chronicle of 1,000 years of political, social, and economic transformation beginning with the dissolution of the classical Mediterranean civilization and ending with the first flowering of the Renaissance. It is also the story of two new religions, Christianity and Islam, both of which were destined to dominate the mind of every person in those new civilizations arising in their wake. This was the great Age of Faith, a time of darkness and a time of enlightenment...a time of lords and vassals, popes and kings, and commerce and cathedrals.

This great history starts with a survey of Christianity, then continues with an exploration of the "dark ages" following the fall of Rome, before proceeding with an explanation of how Europe coped with, and absorbed, the barbarians who overran the Empire.
