Spike includes an interesting Prologue in the book.  “After Puckoon I swore I would never 
write another novel. This is it …”. In the Preface Spike predicts this is the first of a trilogy. 
Another pronouncement that proved incorrect. His war memoirs actually grew to seven 
volumes. 
The book initial print run was 30,000 copies which sold out immediately. In their 1986 
special Classic Edition the publishers, Michael Joseph, state that the book had been 
reprinted eight times. 
Spike had started writing this book in 1967; he had decided to write his autobiography and 
chose to start with his war experience. At some point it became his war memoirs. 
The birth of the book was not without drama. There was an argument over title It’ll All Be 
Over by Christmas favoured by Spike versus Adolf Hitler: My Part in His Downfall 
favoured by Norma Farnes. (Farnes, 2003). There was also the difficult decision of a new 
publisher. The old publisher Jack Hobbs, while initially being taken aback at losing the 
book, actually introduced him to the new publisher Michael Joseph via Dick Douglas-
Boyd. Hobbs becomes an editor. 
This book got the full Milligan publishing treatment. Hardcover, soft cover, American 
hardcover, Book Club edition, Large Print edition, and eventually two French editions. The 
latest edition was published in May 2012 by Penguin who had published the first paperback 
edition in 1973.--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The legendary and iconic figure, Spike Milligan was born at Ahmednagar in India in 1918. He received his first education in a tent in the Hyderabad Sindh desert and graduated from there, through a series of Roman Catholic schools in India and England, to the Lewisham Polytechnic. He then plunged into the world of Show Business, seduced by his first stage appearance, at the age of eight, in the nativity play of his Poona convent school. He began his career as a band musician, but became famous as a humorous scriptwriter and actor in both films and broadcasting. He was the creator, principal writer and performer of the infamous Goon Show. Spike received an honorary CBE in 1992 and Knighthood in 2000. He died in 2002.

# Adolf Hitler

Spike Milligan’s legendary war memoirs are a hilarious and subversive first-hand account of the Second World War, as well as a fascinating portrait of the formative years of this towering comic genius, most famous as writer and star of The Goon Show. They have sold over 4.5 million copies.

Penguin presents the unabridged, downloadable, audiobook edition of Adolf Hitler: My Part in his Downfall written and read by Spike Milligan.

‘At Victoria station the R.T.O. gave me a travel warrant, a white feather and a picture of Hitler marked “This is your enemy”. I searched every compartment, but he wasn’t on the train . . .’

In this, the first of Spike Milligan’s uproarious recollections of life in the army, our hero takes us from the outbreak of war in 1939 (‘it must have been something we said’), through his attempts to avoid enlistment (‘time for my appendicitus, I thought’) and his gunner training in Bexhill (‘There was one drawback. No ammunition’) to the landing at Algiers in 1943 (‘I closed my eyes and faced the sun. I fell down a hatchway’).

Filled with bathos, pathos and gales of ribald laughter, this is a barely sane helping of military goonery and superlative Milliganese.
