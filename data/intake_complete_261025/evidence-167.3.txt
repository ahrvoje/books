## Additional Supplementary Information to my Reply (Reference 1) can be found

below. This includes a point-by-point response to Kate Brown’s letter and a
copy of information which I provided Prof. Brown with in 2016, most of which I
believe was overlooked in Manual for Survival.
Kate Brown’s letter to JRP on Jim Smith’s review of Manual for Survival
Responses from Jim Smith in italics.
I object to James Smith’s deeply-flawed twelve-page, opinion article on my book Manual for Survival
published in your journal (Smith, 2020). The author Dr. Smith is entitled to disagree with the
evidence in my book but his article oversteps the mark and descends into personal, emotional
reactions to my book rather than judging the work on the merits of the 77 pages of archival and
peer-reviewed footnotes in the book.

This is not true. I tried my best to judge the book on the evidence it presents – my review criticises
what I saw as the very one-sided way in which evidence was presented. I believe that evidence and
opinions which disagreed with the thesis of Manual for Survival were not properly represented, as I
highlighted in my review (with clear evidence of this).

Smith writes he was personally offended. He felt I misrepresented him in my book. He writes,
“According to Brown, I was a physicist (used almost as a term of abuse in the context) who didn’t
(which Smith sent to sympathetic activists and journalists) that my omissions were intentional.

Yes, I sent, at his request, the original version of my review to Michael Shellenberger who wrote an
article for Forbes. Michael Shellenberger is a pro-nuclear environmentalist and a Time magazine
Environmental Hero of the Year. I was happy to do this having seen how actively Manual for Survival
was being promoted with reviews by non-scientists which I regarded as ill-informed. Prof Geraldine
Thomas of Imperial College (then Director of the Chernobyl tissue bank) and I also wrote to several
newspapers criticising their reviews of the book.

Incidentally, I also, prior to reading Manual for Survival, sent an article by Kate Brown on Chernobyl4
(which also heavily criticised the scientific community) to BBC International Science Editor, Victoria
Gill. As Victoria Gill was doing a story on our work at Chernobyl, I wanted her to be aware that there
was controversy over health issues, even though I didn’t agree with Kate Brown’s article.

To me, what I saw as the many omissions and errors in Manual for Survival were very surprising in
what purports to be a serious history book about the health consequences of Chernobyl.
