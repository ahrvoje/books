## India: journalists and lawyer faced criminal charges over social media posts on court proceedings

Country
    

  * India

Initial Date
    Sep 16, 2026

Event Description
    

A lawyer, three Muslim journalists and two other social media users have been booked by Uttar Pradesh’s Shamli Police for allegedly sharing “misleading and factless” posts claiming that the Allahabad High Court had reprimanded Shamli Police during proceedings in the case involving 31-year-old Ayush Malik.

The court last week ruled that Malik voluntarily embraced Islam and alleged that his father subsequently subjected him to threats and unlawful confinement.

The FIR was registered at Kotwali Shamli Police Station on September 16 under Section 353(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), following a complaint by Sub-Inspector Mohd Jahangir.

The FIR names advocate Syed Kaif Hasan, journalist Karishma Aziz and Nikhat Ali (@INikhatAli), Wasim Akram Tyagi (@WasimAkramTyagi), The Muslim (@TheMuslimSphere), and Facebook user Nadeem Saifi.

“I do not want an FIR to be filed against that ‘Hindi journalist’ either. But I do have this question for Shamli Police @PoliceShamli: if the basis of the FIR is ‘reprimand,’ then that same ‘reprimand’ word has been used by the ‘Hindi journalist’ as well. When the standard is the same, then why the discrimination? And for what! Another question is whether the police are operating by the Constitution and the law, or by ‘orders from above’!” he added.

What is the Ayush Malik case? According to the police FIR, Ayush Malik’s father, Devraj Malik, complained on June 6 that Chandni Qureshi, her father Islam Qureshi and others had facilitated his son’s conversion to Islam and threatened him and his wife.

Police registered a case under several BNS provisions and the Uttar Pradesh Prohibition of Unlawful Conversion of Religion Act. Chandni Qureshi and Islam Qureshi were arrested on June 7 and granted bail on July 24.
