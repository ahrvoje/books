# The Wisdom of Psychopaths: What Saints, Spies, and Serial Killers Can Teach Us About Success

### Kevin Dutton. FSG/Scientific American, $26 (304p) ISBN 978-0-374-29135-8

Image

Many of us harbor an inner psychopath%E2%80%94and perhaps those who don't, should, says Dutton (Split-Second Persuasion), a Cambridge University research psychologist. Through a series of studies and anecdotes, he demonstrates how for every psychopathic stigma there is a comparably compelling virtue: psychopaths often have a greater capacity for focusing, creativity, and even empathy and altruism. All of this information challenges the idea that psychopaths dwell exclusively at society's outskirts; indeed, Dutton finds psychopathic tendencies in everyone from saints to Secret Service agents to the fictional hero James Bond. Dutton is admirably capable of rendering complicated research into readable and engaging prose. Yet there are times when his repeated use of studies%E2%80%94most conducted in a university or laboratory setting%E2%80%94detracts from his broader analysis of psychopaths within our society. And Dutton's definition of "psychopath" is a little too malleable, often used to refer to a collection of personality traits as opposed to a devastating disorder. We may all possess the potential for the pathology, but our psychopathic paths to success%E2%80%94however fascinating%E2%80%94are still unclear. B&W illus. (Oct.)
