# A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite

## by Adam Higginbotham

The bomb appeared early one morning in an upstairs office of Harvey’s Wagon Wheel Casino near Lake Tahoe, an enigmatic box covered in a bewildering array of switches. A neatly typed letter explained that the box contained 1,000 pounds of dynamite. It was the largest improvised explosive device in American history—and its creator promised to explain how to remove it safely if the casino delivered $3 million by helicopter to a remote landing site in the mountains. “Do not try to move, disarm, or enter the bomb,” the letter warned. “It will explode.”

The bomb maker was one Janos “Big John” Birges, a Hungarian political refugee who had worked his way up from nothing to become a successful entrepreneur in Fresno, California—only to see his life unraveled in middle age by divorce, cancer, and gambling debts. Stranger still, beneath the pyrotechnics lies a poignant story of family. Higginbotham’s skills as a journalist and storyteller left me banging the plate for more.”
—Charles Graeber, author of The Good Nurse

“Of all the spectacular crimes that plagued America at the tail end of the Me Decade, none was more bizarre or more ambitious than the plot to bomb Harvey’s Wagon Wheel Casino. Adam Higginbotham brings this bonkers caper to vivid life in A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite, a tale that explores the very fine line between criminal genius and criminal insanity. You’ll devour this rollicking yarn in one sitting, then spend the next few months regaling your friends with all the astonishing details.”
—Brendan I. Koerner, author of The Skies Belong to Us

## The Recommended Starting Point

Because Adam Higginbotham writes standalone works of narrative nonfiction, there is no chronological storyline or recurring cast of characters to follow. You can read his books in any order you choose. However, for the best experience, we recommend starting with his most celebrated work:

  1. Midnight in Chernobyl (2019): This is Higginbotham's masterwork and the ideal entry point. It showcases his signature ability to blend forensic research, complex science, and deeply personal human stories into a narrative that reads like a tense political thriller.
However, he always translates these complex topics into accessible terms by focusing on the human elements—the pride, the errors, the heroism, and the system failures that define these historic moments.

## Frequently Asked

### Q Do I need to read Adam Higginbotham's books in a specific order?

No. Since all of his books are standalone works of narrative nonfiction covering separate historical events, you can read them in any order. Most readers prefer to start with his most popular book, Midnight in Chernobyl.

### Q What is Adam Higginbotham's best-known book?

His most acclaimed book is Midnight in Chernobyl (2019). It won the 2020 Andrew Carnegie Medal for Excellence in Nonfiction and was widely praised as one of the best investigative accounts of the nuclear disaster.

### Q Is A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite a full-length book?

No, A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite was originally published in 2014 as a long-form digital single by The Atavist Magazine, making it much shorter than his later hardcover books.

### Q Did Midnight in Chernobyl inspire the HBO miniseries?

While the HBO series Chernobyl and Higginbotham's book were released around the same time in 2019 and share similar historical sources, the TV show was not an official adaptation of his book. However, the book serves as an excellent companion read for fans of the show who want the fully documented facts.

### Q What awards has Challenger won?

Adam Higginbotham's 2024 book Challenger won the prestigious Kirkus Prize for Nonfiction and the National Book Critics Circle Award for Nonfiction.

By 1980, he owed hundreds of thousands of dollars to Harvey’s. And he had roped his two teenage sons - who were as eager to please their father as they were terrified of him - into a plot to get the money back.
But the bomb he planted in the casino that August wasn’t just an extortion scheme. It was a brilliant feat of engineering - an intricate and deadly puzzle that Birges hoped would prove once and for all just how badly the world had underestimated him. In A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite, Adam Higginbotham draws from interviews with federal agents and Birges’ co-conspirators - as well as never-before-released FBI records - to tell the story of the race to stop one of history’s most bizarre extortion plots. By turns action-packed and darkly hilarious, A Thousand Pounds of Dynamite is an engrossing tale of genius at its most deranged.
