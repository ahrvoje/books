# Rent a person who does nothing: the idea, explained

In 2018 a man in Tokyo started charging people to simply be there. He has now been booked thousands of times. Here's what "renting a person who does nothing" actually means, why it works, and where it goes next.

Published 20 September 2026 · By the Nothing team

## The short version

Renting a person who does nothing means paying for someone's presence instead of their effort. You book a person; they turn up; they exist next to you. They won't advise you, fix anything, or want anything from you. That last part — wanting nothing from you — turns out to be the whole product.

## Who is Shoji Morimoto?

Shoji Morimoto is a man from Tokyo who, in June 2018, posted on Twitter that he was available to rent as a "person who does nothing" (Rental Nan mo Shinai Hito in Japanese). His pitch was disarmingly plain: he would go where you asked and do nothing, apart from eating, drinking and offering "a simple reply". You paid a flat fee and covered his train fare and any food.

Therapy is care. Dating is romance. Friendship is reciprocal. A rented Nothing is none of those things and never pretends to be. That clarity is why it feels safe to book, and why it works for people who would never book anything else.

## What it is not

Morimoto's rules have been consistent from day one, and they matter. He does not do work. He does not give advice. He does not do anything that requires effort. And it is strictly platonic — no physical contact, no romance, no exceptions. Those boundaries aren't small print; they're the reason the whole thing holds together.

## Where the idea goes next: Nothing

Morimoto's model has one obvious limitation: there is one of him, in one city, booked by direct message. We built Nothing to make the same idea available in more places — with the things a stranger-meets-stranger service needs at scale.

  * The same offer. A verified, friendly person who does nothing with you. Sit, walk, wait. That's it.
  * The same rules. Strictly platonic. 18+. No advice, no effort, no chores. Public places only.
  * Flat pricing, Morimoto-style. One price per session length — 1 hour, 2 hours or 4 hours — set for your city and shown before you book. You cover tickets, transport and food during the session.
  * Real safety tooling. ID + selfie verification with human approval, in-app messaging, and an SOS button for both people that shows the local emergency number, ends the session, refunds the client and alerts our safety team.
  * Fair pay. Nothings keep 85% of every session, paid weekly, and can decline any booking without giving a reason.

We're launching city by city, worldwide — each city opens as its waitlist fills, wherever in the world you are.

# Rental Person Who Does Nothing: summary, review and the ideas that matter

Shoji Morimoto's memoir is a short, strange, quietly profound book about a man who got paid to exist. Here's what it covers, what it gets right, where it's thin, and who should pick it up.

Published 23 September 2026 · By the Nothing team · Nothing is inspired by Morimoto's work and is not affiliated with him or his publishers.

In one line: a memoir-slash-casebook about being paid to be present, which ends up being a book about how little most people actually need from each other — and how rarely they get it.

## The premise

In 2018, Shoji Morimoto posted on Twitter that he was available to hire as a "rental person who does nothing". He'd go where you asked, eat and drink, give simple replies — and that was all. Clients covered his travel and any expenses, plus a fee.

The idea, by his own account, came out of a period of feeling like he wasn't useful in a conventional job. Rather than fight that, he took it to its logical end: what if not doing anything was the offer?

## From the book to the street

The obvious limit of Morimoto's model is that there's one of him, in one city, booked by direct message. We built Nothing to take the same idea — presence, with firm boundaries — and make it bookable in more places, with the safety tooling a stranger-meets-stranger service needs.

Same offer: a verified person who sits with you, walks with you or waits with you, and does nothing else. Same rules: strictly platonic, no effort, no advice, public places only. Added: ID + selfie verification, in-app messaging, and an SOS button for both people. For the longer version of his story, read rent a person who does nothing: the idea, explained.

Today, I’m starting a ‘rent a person who does nothing’ service . . . Except for very simple conversation, I’m afraid I can do nothing.

Shoji Morimoto was constantly being told that he was a ‘do-nothing’ because he lacked initiative. Dispirited and unemployed, it occurred to him that if he was so good at doing nothing, perhaps he could turn it into a business. And with one tweet, he began his business of renting himself out . . . to do nothing.

For 10,000 yen or roughly £60, Rental Person provides a fascinating service to the lonely and socially anxious. This book details some of his thousands of true life adventures: from sitting with a client undergoing surgery and accompanying a newly-divorced client to her favourite restaurant, to visiting the site of a client’s suicide attempt. Rental Person is dependable, non-judgmental and committed to remaining a stranger and the curious encounters he shares are revelatory about both Japanese society and human psychology.

In Rental Person Who Does Nothing, Morimoto chronicles his extraordinary experiences in his unique line of work and reflects on how we consider relationships, jobs and family in our search for meaningful connection and purpose in life.

Un hombre se alquila por 70 euros por "no hacer nada" y tiene miles de clientes
Date: Sun Jan 17 07:06:35 2021 UTC

Shoji Morimoto es un japonés de 37 años que se gana la vida alquilándose "para no hacer nada", según informa el diario Independent. Para arrendarle, hay que pagar 10.000 yenes (unos 70 euros) más los gastos de viaje y comidas. Aunque tal y como avisa él mismo, no hará nada más que "comer, beber y dar una respuesta sencilla".
Gracias a su actividad, ha obtenido una enorme cantidad de seguidores y miles de clientes. Su primer servicio fue en junio de 2018 tras publicar el siguiente tuit: "Me ofrezco en alquiler, como una persona que no hace nada. ¿Le resulta difícil entrar a una tienda por su cuenta? ¿Echas de menos un jugador en tu equipo? ¿Necesitas que alguien te guarde un lugar? No puedo hacer nada más que cosas fáciles".
Al principio no cobraba
Inicialmente, ofrecía sus servicios de forma gratuita, pero ahora cobra para disuadir a aquellos que le hacen perder el tiempo y para reducir el volumen de solicitudes que le llegan. Pese a ello, Morimoto afirma que tiene tres o cuatro clientes al día, y en total acumula alrededor de 3.000 desde que comenzó.
Entre las razones alegadas por aquellas personas que lo alquilan, destacan el aburrimiento, la soledad o que simplemente quieren que les escuchen. Así, ha sido contratado para almorzar, acompañar a alguien que solicita el divorcio o para posar en fotografías publicadas en Instagram.
Su labor
Morimoto describió en el periódico The Mainichi su actividad: "No soy un amigo ni un conocido. Estoy libre de las cosas molestas que acompañan a las relaciones, pero puedo aliviar los sentimientos de soledad de las personas".
"Personalmente, no me gusta que otras personas me animen. Me molesta cuando la gente simplemente me dice que siga perseverando. Cuando alguien está intentando hacer algo, creo que lo mejor es facilitarle las cosas manteniéndose a su lado", añadió en el citado medio.
