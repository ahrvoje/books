## Abstract

Memory: A Very Short Introduction explores how memories are stored and why some events from childhood seem as vivid as if they happened yesterday, while others fade. It delves into the brain processes that make memory possible and how they change with age. This VSI also examines why memory sometimes goes wrong and how easily others can manipulate our recollections. It shows how memory plays a crucial role in daily life and learning. The edition includes updates that reflect the latest findings on sleep and healthy ageing, offering new insights into the science of memory.

Keywords: memory, childhood, brain, age, recollections, manipulate, sleep, science, healthy

## Abstract

Memory: A Very Short Introduction weaves together case-studies, anecdotes, literature and philosophy, and the latest research in neuroscience and psychology to address these and many other important questions about the science of memory — how it works, and why we can't live without it. Memories are an integral part of being human, and they underpin our sense of who we are. Many people report being able to remember events from childhood as if they happened yesterday, but not what they did last week. Why does memory seem to work well sometimes and not others? Can memory be improved or manipulated by psychological techniques or even ‘brain implants’? How does memory grow and change as we age? And what of so-called ‘recovered’ memories?

Keywords: cued recall, encoding specificity, episodic memory, explicit memory, eyewitness testimony, free recall, implicit memory, levels of processing, memory, mild cognitive impairment, semantic memory
