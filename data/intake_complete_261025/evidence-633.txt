## Abstract

Forensic Psychology: A Very Short Introduction discusses the aspects of psychology that are relevant to the legal and criminal process. It includes explanations of criminal behaviour and criminality, including the role of mental disorder in crime, and discusses how forensic psychology contributes to helping investigate the crime and catching the perpetrators. It also explains how psychologists provide guidance to those involved in civil and criminal court proceedings and what expert testimony can be provided by a psychologist about the offender at the trial. Finally, this VSI examines how forensic psychology is used, particularly in prisons, to help in the management, treatment and rehabilitation of offenders, once they have been convicted.

Keywords: family, forensic psychology, information, mental disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, thematic apperception test

## Abstract

What is forensic psychology? ‘The excitement and challenge of forensic psychology’ provides some answers to this question. The term ‘forensic psychology’ is the application to all aspects of the law and management of crime and criminals, through professional practice, of principles, theories, and methods derived from the scientific and clinical studies of human actions and experience. Psychology in forensic treatment settings and in the prison and probation service is considered as well as the challenges to forensic psychology and the growing subdisciplines of prison psychology, investigative psychology, legal psychology, and forensic aspects of clinical psychology. Clear differentiation from criminology, forensic psychiatry, and forensic science is also outlined.

Keywords: criminology, forensic psychiatry, forensic psychology, mens rea, mental disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, science

## Abstract

‘Psychology and legal proceedings’ examines the broader issues of psychological contributions to the legal process. The psychologists' role in determining insanity of a defendant, false confessions, and recovered memories is discussed. Psychological ‘syndromes’ that have controversially found their way into legal proceedings such as post-traumatic stress disorder, battered woman syndrome, and rape trauma syndrome are considered. The evidence given by forensic psychologists as expert witnesses derives largely from the assessment of individuals using clinical interviews and diagnostic instruments. Psychologists' advice may now also reach out to other aspects of court procedure such as jury selection and suggesting ways in which information should be presented in court.

Keywords: insanity, post-traumatic stress disorder, psychology, syndrome, trauma

## Abstract

‘How to make a criminal’ shows that explanations of criminal behaviour and criminality are central to forensic psychology and considers what gives rise to criminality. It provides a range of accounts for such behaviour. These are biological, psychological, and social explanations; mental disorder and the use of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders and International Classification of Diseases; psychopathy; addiction and substance abuse; criminal networks; violence; emotions; and criminal narratives. The conclusion is that a criminal is not just a particular type of person, but that the criminal context for that individual — family and associates as well as a broader society and culture — is critical.

Keywords: addiction, family, mental disorder, narratives, psychology, psychopathy, substance abuse

## Abstract

‘Experts in court’ reviews the contributions that forensic psychologists make as expert witnesses. Expert evidence is given to assist the court in its decision, whether the expert agrees with that decision or not. The role that forensic psychologists play in court proceedings depends considerably on the particular jurisdiction and legal context in which they are operating and the legal questions they are asked to answer. All of their contributions utilize theories, methods, principles, and procedures that are unique to forensic psychologists and their clinical experiences. The limits of expert evidence and psychological assessment procedures, such as the Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory, are discussed.

Keywords: forensic psychology, Sigmund Freud, mental disorder, personality inventory, thematic apperception test

Forensic Psychology: A Very Short Introduction 
By David Canter 
 
Lie detection, offender profiling, insanity in the law, the minds of serial killers, and 
many other topics that fill news and fiction are all aspects of the rapidly developing 
area of Forensic Psychology. David Canter shows how these often controversial 
topics bridge the gaps between academics and practitioners, behavioural sciences, and 
the law.
