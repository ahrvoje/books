#### kmardahl's review

2.0

* * *

I finally finished this book. I really really wanted to read a history book about the Middle Ages. It was part of my plan to refresh the history lessons of my school days. Maybe this book took on too much. 1000 years is a lot! I cannot tell you how many pages there are because this is only found in a 19-hour audiobook format.
There are three authors listed: Crane Brinton, John Christopher, and Robert Wolff. None of them list this book in their bibliographies! Strange. Only on Wolff's wikipedia page did I find a book called "A History of Civilization" that listed the other two men as authors. Basically, I had a strong suspicion that someone cobbled together bits of other books and called it all "The History of the Middle Ages". Granted, my reading stretched out over such a long time that I have probably forgotten how other parts of the book flowed. The very last section of the book was about 1 hour long and was called a conclusion, but I don't feel it concluded all 1000 years. It seemed to only conclude certain aspects of those 1000 years. There is no printed version available that I can examine. Google reveals hardly anything about this book.
I thought about finding a paper copy when I was a few hours into the book. That was when I first became suspicious. OK a book has the right to exist solely in audio form. The book was apparently first published by something called Audio Connoisseur in 2004. On April 1st no less! Crane Brinton, the first author listed, died in 1968. I think any book that he wrote and published in his lifetime would have been on paper. While writing this review, I tried once more to search for some background information. I discovered that Prentice-Hall had a history series where these three authors published a two-volume book called A History of Civilization. That made me go to Abebooks.com where I found a major clue. It looks like the works of these three authors were shuffled and re-shuffled into ... text books. None of them covered just the Middle Ages, but I am guessing these books had a modular structure that meant someone just chopped and re-shuffled! So I read a text book? True, I wanted to refresh my school lessons, but I thought I had graduated from a text book to a nice, quality history book for pleasure.
When I see that some of the books were written in 1955 or the early 1960s, I can better understand what I consider the biased tone of the book. I am not a historian and certainly no Middle Ages expert, but I sensed a strong bias of some kind throughout. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 

A history of the Middle Ages, 300-1500

Author:

John M. Riddle (Author)

Summary:This text covers the Middle Ages from the classical era to the late medieval period. The author provides an analysis of the rulers, wars, and events, both natural and human, that defined the medieval era. Taking a broad geographical perspective, he includes northern and eastern Europe, Byzantium, and the Islamic states. He shows how each offered values and institutions that presaged modernity. In addition to a thorough chronological narrative, the text offers humanizing features. Each chapter opens with a theme-setting vignette about daily life and introduces key controversies and themes in historiography by discussing the work of a prominent medieval historian. This book immerses readers in the medieval world, an era that shaped the foundation for the modern world
