thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476223664332976135" dir="auto"> Semiconductors can be a hard industry to understand, especially for investors without a technical background. Here are a few of the books that really helped me understand semiconductors better. In order of approachability - a 🧵
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476224146262614016" dir="auto"> 1/ In the beginning there was Fairchild. I think that is the best way to start. Makers of the Microchip by Christophe Lécuyer is the best book to begin with IMO.
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476224524832100362" dir="auto"> 2/ Fairchild of course birthed Intel - which is where "The Intel Trinity" by Michael Malone transition well into. Intel of course is *the company* in semiconductor history and this was one of the better books I read about this topic.
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476225369569214473" dir="auto"> 3/ After Intel I think it's worth looking at it's Texas neighbor - Texas Instruments. "Engineering the World - Stories from the first 75 years by Caleb Pirtile" did the best job at covering TI's long and storied history IMO.
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476226015097667590" dir="auto"> 4/ After TI - I think it's fair to start to look East. The book that is frustratingly academic but filled with information is "Tiger Technology by John Matthews & Dong-Sung Cho" Filled with amazing information about the histories of Taiwanese & Korean semi industries
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476226886728667143" dir="auto"> 5/ Now I think it's time to get a bit more specific. In particular I think it's time to read Fabless by @DanielNenni / @paulmclellan Fabless IMO is one of the best books of the bunch, and the last chapter's predictions hold up shockingly well.
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476227559826374662" dir="auto"> @DanielNenni @paulmclellan 6/ This is where the reading track is starting to fall apart IMO. I'm going to recommend a book if you can get your hands on it called "Information for Everyone: The Applied Materials Story" This is a pretty good history book on Applied and one of the few I've found.
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476228568707485704" dir="auto"> 7/ Given how hot Automotive is right now - I really liked Sensors in Automotive by @junkoyoshida It's just a really easy to read no-jargon series of interviews that really brought me up to speed on automotive sensors
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476235047669796867" dir="auto"> @junkoyoshida 8/ 400 level classes begin now! I can't pretend like I understood everything - but I did benefit massively from comprehending ~30% of these books. Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach The most recent editions foreword blew my mind and started me down the rabbit hole
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476235399831998469" dir="auto"> @junkoyoshida 9/ Semiconductor Packaging: Materials Interaction and Reliability This book is really good for an overview of all the basic packaging types and pros and cons. In a world of flipchip / ball bonding / tcb / and a lot more this one was crucial. I prob need to reread
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476235623174483973" dir="auto"> @junkoyoshida 10/ I wrote my lithography primer of ASML after I read this book. It pretty much taught me a lot about litho from the DUV era + coating / spin process steps. Fundamental Principles of Optical Lithography: The Science of Microfabrication
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476235992680050695" dir="auto"> @junkoyoshida 11/ HONORABLE MENTIONS! Some of these I know will be good - some I am in the midst of reading - some are only slightly relevant. (In midst of reading) Handbook of Silicon Semiconductor Metrology
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476236123697516545" dir="auto"> 12/ just a good book anyone with an interest in tech and investing should read Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476236809491394566" dir="auto"> 13/ The Big Score: The Billion Dollar Story of Silicon Valley To read but Malone was up earlier on the list
thread#showTweet" data-screenname="fabknowledge" data-tweet="1476237291408527366" dir="auto"> END / There's probably a ton more books to be read over time. I am slowly adding to my list on Goodreads if you want to keep up. goodreads.com/review/list/27… Also if you don't want to read hard books on semis - always consider following along to my substack in my bio.
