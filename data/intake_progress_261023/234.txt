Mechanical Engineering
The following books from the Library's collections examine the history of mechanical engineering. The bibliography contains a selection of books for a wide audience. Each item links to fuller bibliographic information in the Library of Congress Online Catalog. Links to additional online content are provided when available.
To find additional books on this topic, search the online catalog using the following Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH):
Mechanical Engineering - History
Landmarks in Mechanical Engineering by ASME International History and Heritage Call Number: TJ23 .L35 1997
ISBN: 1557530939
Published/Created: 1996
The book describes, and where possible illustrates, historic mechanical engineering landmarks, representing the accomplishments of mechanical engineers over the past 250 years, from the steam engine of Thomas Newcomen (1712), which launched the Industrial Revolution, to the Saturn V rocket (1967).
Mechanical Engineering by Don Herweck Call Number: TJ147 .H47 2009
ISBN: 9780756539528
Published/Created: 2008
This book discusses the field of mechanical engineering.
Naval Engineering and American Sea Power by R. W. King Call Number: VA55 .N24 2000
ISBN: 0933852738
Published/Created: 2000
This book is comprised of selected narratives aimed at a wide audience of those who are interested in the history of maritime engineering and its implications.
Various and Ingenious Machines (2 Vols. ) by Bryan Lawton Call Number: TJ15 .L39 2004
ISBN: 9004136096
Published/Created: 2004
This two-volume work describes the early history of mechanical engineering from prehistory, when mining and agriculture first appear, to the beginnings of industrialization. The books are divided into four major sections: power generation and transport in the first volume; manufacturing technology and weapons technology in the second volume.
