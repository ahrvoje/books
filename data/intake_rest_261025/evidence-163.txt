In other households people might shout, " The bird has escaped from its cage. Quick, shut the window?" Not so in Konrad Lorenz' family. With them the cry has always been, "For goodness' sake shut the window. the cockatoo--or raven--or monkey-- is trying to get in!" Dr. Lorenz goes on to explain that the keeping of higher animals in a state of unrestricted freedom has always been his specialty. In view of the wealth of experiences related in King Solomon' Ring, that, certainly, is an understatement.

# The King Solomon Ring Story Behind "This Too Shall Pass"

August 25, 2026

The King Solomon ring story goes like this. A king wants a sentence that will be true in every season of his life, so his advisors give him a ring engraved with "this too shall pass."

When he reads it in grief, it comforts him. When he reads it at a celebration, it humbles him. One sentence, doing two opposite jobs.

The story is real folklore. The ring itself is not a historical object, and no ancient text records Solomon wearing one.

## The oldest version is Persian, not Jewish

The earliest written form comes from 12th century Persian poetry. Attar of Nishapur tells it in his Ilāhī-Nāma as "The Padishah and the Ring," and Sanai records a version too.

In Attar's telling there is no Solomon. There is a powerful, unnamed king who asks his wise men for a ring that will make him happy when he is sad.

The sages debate, then hand him something plain with a single line etched on it. The oldest surviving illustrated manuscript of that poem, from 1458, sits in the National Library of Israel.
