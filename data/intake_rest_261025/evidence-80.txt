45. Alex's Adventures in Numberland, by Alex Bellos (bookmark)

Published by: Bloomsbury, 2011

Look, any book that takes me on a guided tour of pivotal moments in the history of mathematics is going to be like catnip for me, but the fact that this book sometimes also goes by the wonderful title of Here's Looking at Euclid cements its place in my favourite books list. This is the sort of book that gets me thoroughly engrossed and I'd read the whole thing (including the appendices, of course) in a couple of days. The only problem I had in making my way that rapidly through this compendium of weird and wonderful characters was that I struggled to resist the temptation to sit down and check all of the mathematical examples for myself (and I wasn't particularly successful in doing that). After all, these days I have an entire shelf full of books about maths in my library and I still have both a straight slide rule and a circular one (which, as Mr Bellos explains, is known as a "whiz wheel") tucked away in a drawer upstairs. We didn't have pocket calculators back when I got an "A" in my maths O-level.
