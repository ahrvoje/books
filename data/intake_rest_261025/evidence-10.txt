# Nose Dive: A Field Guide to the World's Smells

by Harold McGee Author

Brought to us by the award-winning author, Harold McGee, Nose Dive, is a fascinating exploration of history, food, and our larger world by way of smell. What are the molecules that provoke are senses in the medicinal smell of sea urchins and daffodils, the sharp citrus present in many ales and coriander, or the agreeable sweetness of roses or vanilla? A highly accessible and entertaining, yet scholarly volume, Nose Dive is a history of the world as much as an invitation to smell our communities and ourselves.

Back in 2004, San Francisco-based food writer Harold McGee finished the second edition of a book he first published 20 years earlier, and which has become seminal work in the field of gastronomy. Titled On Food and Cooking, it is an encyclopedic look at the science behind food. Heston Blumenthal of The Fat Duck (in Bray, Berkshire) says it was the most influential book in his career, which from a chef of his reputation is praise indeed. Some 16 years later, McGee looks to have done it again, with this substantial reference work on smell.

To call it an encyclopedia would be doing it a disservice, because Nose Dive is a work of stunning originality. Whether it’s coffee, baking bread, frying onions or washing powder, we live surrounded by a myriad of aromas that influence and direct us in countless ways every day. But what are smells exactly and how do they work? And how can we pin them down in words?

Legendary food scientist Harold McGee has spent the last decade researching the science of our most overlooked sense, and this book is the fascinating result. From our own specific human scents to those of the world around us, *Nose Dive* offers a powerful encyclopaedia of everything olfactory – that every cook and gourmet will need to have. An instant culinary and sensory classic.
