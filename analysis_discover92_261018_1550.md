## Source 50 — Gunpowder and Galleys

**John F. Guilmartin Jr., 1974. 93.30 — Recommend; moderate confidence.**

The central question is why an apparently inferior technology can remain the best choice within its operating system. [Daniel Moran's 2004 assessment](https://ciaotest.cc.columbia.edu/olj/si/si_3_3/si_3_3_mod01.pdf) shows how the book connects hull capacity, coastal bases, scarce artillery, skilled crews, prices and competing land commitments. Its value for this reader lies in constraints and resource allocation: superior isolated performance need not produce superior strategic results.

**Strongest objection:** the title promises more about gunpowder than the book delivers. [Richon's 1975 review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/outre_0300-9513_1975_num_62_228_1862_t1_0519_0000_1?pageId=T1_520) notes missing treatment of powder manufacture and raw-material supply. This is a real gap in the production chain. [LibraryThing's reader review](https://www.librarything.com/work/21974) also reports dry passages and disruptive endnotes. The inaccessible Scammell review cannot be treated as either approval or refutation; its visible footnotes alone do not establish the argument. These limits support E=18 and N=2, not near-perfect evidence or effortless reading.

**Hard exclusions:** nonfiction, so rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes with moderate confidence. This is a sustained historical explanation, alternating battle narratives with causal analysis. Technical appendices support that explanation; the main work does not train readers to operate weapons, design ships or apply a specialist method. Richon's [first-page assessment](https://www.persee.fr/doc/outre_0300-9513_1975_num_62_228_1862_t1_0519_0000_1) identifies interest beyond naval specialists, including economics and sociology. Its academic origins and reference value do not by themselves make it an excluded technical reference. The boundary is closer here than for Landes.

**Vetoes:** no serious V1 failure is established by the inspected evidence; the missing supply analysis remains a limitation. No evidence of allegiance-dependent reasoning, a manifesto or activism establishes V2/V5. Reusable constraint analysis defeats V3. Its mature historical argument does not depend on hype, so V4 does not apply. Absence-of-veto confidence: moderate.

## Source 51 — The Invisible Weapon

**Daniel R. Headrick, 1991. 92.30 — Recommend; moderate confidence.**

This is a history of how communications networks become strategic assets. The [Oxford description and contents](https://academic.oup.com/book/47948) connect cable and radio development with commercial rivalry, diplomacy, intelligence and war. The relevant lesson is that faster communication changes available choices and vulnerabilities; it does not dictate a single political outcome.

**Strongest objection:** the new insight is uneven. The [indexed text of Eliot Cohen's 1993 Foreign Affairs review](https://www.scribd.com/document/1053035186/Review-2-the-Invisible-Weapon-Telecommunications-and-International-Politics) welcomes the contribution but says the later World War II intelligence sections add little new. Only a fragment of that review was available. [Mark's signed reader review](https://app.thestorygraph.com/book_reviews/9f03715a-9aff-444d-99aa-c352cdf4ebf6) finds peacetime political control less well demonstrated than wartime effects. That reader judgment is weaker evidence than a specialist assessment, but it identifies a sensible limitation. The same review appears on Goodreads; it is counted once.

**Hard exclusions:** rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes with moderate confidence: the chapter sequence is comparative historical explanation for readers of technology and international relations. It is not communications training, a cryptographic manual or a technical reference catalogue. Technical development serves the historical argument.

**Vetoes:** no inspected evidence establishes material central errors, ideological dependence or activism (V1/V2/V5). Infrastructure dependence, control points and substitution provide transferable value (V3). The historical account has durable value independent of present news (V4). Absence-of-veto confidence: moderate.

## Source 52 — Revolution in Time

**David S. Landes, 1983. 92.30 — Recommend; moderate confidence.**

The book treats precision as an economic and institutional achievement. [Hobsbawm's 1983 review](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1983/12/08/on-the-watch/) explains its movement between time measurement, industrial development and shifts in manufacturing advantage. [Golinski's review, printed page 51](https://radicalphilosophyarchive.com/issue-files/rp42_reviews.pdf), supports its ability to connect cultural demand, technical improvement and production.

**Strongest objection:** one industry's history cannot carry every claim about modernization. Hobsbawm questions this generalization and finds cultural history less visible in the long final industrial section. Golinski considers the middle technical section less interesting than the discussion of conceptual change. For this physicist reader, that section still supports D=9: the precision problem and its engineering constraints are part of the desired content. N=3 assesses the clear organization and prose praised by both reviewers, not an absence of demanding passages.

**Hard exclusions:** both pass, high eligibility confidence. Its three-part architecture is a historical argument about finding, keeping and making time. It neither teaches watch repair nor supplies a specialist design method. Descriptions of mechanisms and an appendix do not change its dominant purpose.

**Vetoes:** no V1 failure is established; broad cultural explanations retain uncertainty and lose evidence points. The concentration on European industrial development requires scrutiny under V2, but is not by itself proof that the argument depends on cultural allegiance. The inspected specialist reviews identify substantive comparison and explanation, not a manifesto. V5 is not established. Industry migration, standards and technical adaptation supply V3 value; the history does not depend on a fashionable forecast (V4). Absence-of-veto confidence: moderate.

## Source 53 — How Economics Shapes Science

**Paula Stephan, 2012. 90.21 — Recommend, below the requested threshold; moderate confidence.**

The book explains how research incentives shape careers, laboratories and the production of knowledge. [Finneran's review](https://issues.org/books/) particularly supports its handling of uncertainty: desirable outcomes alone do not establish that a funding decision was optimal. It examines alternatives and does not pretend that the best grant size or research budget is known. This supports E=19.

**Strongest objection:** the scope is narrower than its title. [Tyfield's specialist review](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/document/50595) praises the evidence and balance, while arguing that a focus on publicly funded research leaves industrial science and the changing relation between science and the wider economy insufficiently developed. This supports S=16. [Wilsdon](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/books/how-economics-shapes-science/419590.article) finds international relevance but confirms the U.S. focus. Period-specific funding and workforce data also justify R=8. This is the least suitable of the four for someone who wants to minimize institutional administration.

**Hard exclusions:** rule 1 passes. Rule 2 passes with moderate confidence. The [contents and chapter extracts](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt2jbqd1) form an explanatory account of incentives, research resources, labor markets and economic contribution. They are not a sequence of grant-writing instructions or economics exercises. A specialist audience for parts of the material does not turn the work into technical training.

**Vetoes:** no serious V1/V2 failure is established. Tyfield explicitly recognizes the treatment of evidence that complicates a one-sided critique. Funding recommendations do not make the dominant explanatory account a political manifesto or activism book; V5 is not established. Research incentives provide V3 payload. The dated figures lower durability but do not make the central mechanisms depend on hype (V4). Absence-of-veto confidence: moderate.
