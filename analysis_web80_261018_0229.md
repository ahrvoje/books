## Scope and method

The user requested discovery across the web rather than analysis of a supplied thread. The search covered industrial and economic history, engineering and risk, computing institutions, finance, strategic interaction, and scientific organization. Broad discovery used [Five Books technology](https://fivebooks.com/category/technology/) and [economic-history selections](https://fivebooks.com/category/economics/economic-history/), together with open web searches for relevant histories and critical reviews. Title-specific searches then checked purpose, structure, editions, reader response and serious objections.

The assessed corpus is the **30 named works**, each attached to a book-specific source in SOURCES.md. Unrelated titles in navigation, recommendation widgets, bibliographies, search results or advertisements were discovery leads, not an instruction to ingest those entire catalogues. This is a broad, bounded research pass; it does not claim to exhaust the web or all possible 80-plus books. No discussion thread was used as a corpus, so no claim of complete comment expansion is made.

The report cites **98 distinct evidence pages from 53 domains**, plus reader-rating pages. Publisher descriptions and available contents establish intended audience and structure; reviews and research papers test claims and expose objections. A publisher's endorsement is not independent validation. Only accessible text or identified search-index extracts was used. Full books were not read cover to cover during this project, and inaccessible review bodies are not presented as inspected.

All 30 titles were checked against the three status files and searched across prior Markdown and assessment files. None was already recorded. There are therefore no old scores to copy, average or change. Historical analyses remain unchanged. [Source 10's thematic policy](analysis_pastedlists_261018_0118.md) and the [source 11 assessments](analysis_pastefollow_261018_0130.md) were read to keep the distinction between a technological case study and general national history consistent.

Hard exclusions were applied before scoring. Every retained work is nonfiction and has an explanatory or argumentative structure beyond specialist training. Two otherwise relevant books fail rule 2. Veto review considers central factual or causal failure, allegiance in place of evidence, lack of transferable content, and dependence on hype. Disagreement alone is not a veto. Each book's limits remain in its score.

## Best starting choices

Read **Networks of Power (92)** for the joint development of engineering, firms, finance and institutions; **Arms and Influence (92)** for commitment, credibility and bargaining; and **The Gifts of Athena (91)** for the relation between useful knowledge and sustained industrial growth. These cover different mechanisms and have less overlap than reading several histories of computing in succession. The individual assessments below give the supporting sources and objections.

Next choose **Micromotives and Macrobehavior (91)** for aggregate effects, **The Economic Weapon (90)** for economic coercion, or **Money Changes Everything (89)** for financial institutions. **Dealers of Lightning (88)** is the strongest new trade narrative of research organization in this set.

### Source 13 — The Economic Weapon

**Nicholas Mulder · 2022.** **90/100 · Recommend; score confidence moderate.** New work; no prior assessment or source number was replaced.

**Hard-exclusion check:** Comparative archival history for readers of international affairs. It explains the origin and use of sanctions; it does not train sanctions practitioners.

**Evidence used:** [Yale: description and paperback](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300262520/the-economic-weapon/) · [Author excerpt](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/2022/02/24/the-history-of-economic-sanctions-as-a-tool-of-war/) · [Christopher Coyne review](https://www.independent.org/tir/2023-spring/the-economic-weapon/) · [Naval Institute review](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2022/july/book-reviews). The following allocations are this review's judgments based on those sources.

**Breakdown:** E/M/U/S/D/R/F/A/N/P/T = **18/15/14/12/7/9/7/4/2/2 / 0 = 90**.

E18/A4: Archival research and several forms of historical evidence support the account. The interwar cases do not identify the independent effect of every sanction.

M15/U14/S12/F7: Trade dependence, finance, deterrence and expectations connect economic pressure to military choices. Strong transfer to supply-chain and strategic-risk analysis.

D7/R9: Substantial institutional detail costs reading time. The interaction of coercion and economic adaptation remains useful beyond current sanctions disputes.

N2/P2: A sustained, careful argument, with more institutional exposition than narrative momentum.

**Thematic deduction: 0.** International economic and military history. Colonial and humanitarian consequences are part of the causal account, not a predominant excluded theme.

**Counterargument and veto decision:** Do not turn the interwar record into a universal claim that sanctions always fail or cause war. No serious veto is established; confidence moderate.
